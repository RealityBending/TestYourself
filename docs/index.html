01 · The problem #

The trouble with surveys

The instruments are rarely what fails. The answering is.

Psychology runs on questionnaires, nearly all asked the same way: a page of radio buttons on a platform built for the person writing the study, not the person answering it. No validation survives careless answers.

For the participantNothing makes it worth finishing

  • Built for the researcher

    Qualtrics, Gorilla, jsPsych: good at building a study, indifferent to taking one. Grid after grid, then a thank-you.

  • Boredom and fatigue

    The same item is answered worse at the end of a long battery, and those who stop halfway are not a random half.

  • Nothing back

    An hour of honest answers buys a debriefing sheet: no reason to care how you answer, come back or tell anyone.

For the dataSo what comes back is weak

  • Careless responding

    Often a tenth of an online sample or more (Meade & Craig, 2012), and enough to make unrelated scales correlate (Huang et al., 2015).

  • Jingle and jangle

    Most scales are used once or twice (Elson et al., 2023) and seldom side by side, so whether grit is just conscientiousness is rarely checked.

  • Small, closed datasets

    A few scales, a few hundred people, a file kept on a drive. Pooling them later means reconciling what never matched.

02 · The answer #

What the Abyss is

A survey people want to finish.

The questionnaires are ordinary validated ones, asked as written. Everything around them is designed for the person answering, so that the last question is read as carefully as the first.

The aim: high-quality, re-usable, open datasets, with many measures answered by the same people, so how far they overlap can be seen.

For the participantReasons to keep going

  • Staged progress

    Levels, dressed as a dive: a depth gauge, landmarks passing, the seabed and the rock below.

  • Feedback

    Each level closes on a figure drawn from the answers just given: a body, a hill, a sea, a wheel.

  • Agency

    After the first level, participants pick which part of themselves to test next, from two or three cards.

  • Collection

    Each level mints a badge onto a shelf, and a whole-run profile fills in as the levels do.

For the dataAnswers worth keeping

  • Asking back

    Every reading can be agreed or disagreed with, every level rated: the Barnum effect, caught in the act.

  • Quiet quality control

    Attention checks, response times and an honesty question, recorded for every level and shown to nobody.

  • Nothing lost

    Answers are saved as they are given, and a run left partway picks up where it stopped.

  • Sharing

    Results go out as a link or an image that opens into the test: recruitment by word of mouth, filed as such.

Built to be changed: the questions are data rather than code, and each project asks its own levels from its own link.

03 · The first project #

Project: MINT follow-up

A project defines a list of mandatory, priority levels that every participant starts with, before the optional ones. The aim of this first project is to collect more data on the MINT and to validate a scale of attitudes to AI, the BAIT.

MINT

Multidimensional Interoceptive Traits questionnaire · Makowski et al.

How people sense the inside of their own body, as a trait.

Level 2 · Brain-Body Axis

BAIT

Beliefs about Artificial Intelligence Technology · Makowski et al.

What people believe AI can make, and how they feel about it.

Level 3 · AI Attitudes

Core · levels 1–4, every participant

Optional · levels 5–9, the participant's choice
